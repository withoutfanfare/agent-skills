# Copydesk

Good copy is specific. "Hand-printed on 300 gsm cotton paper, signed by
the artist" sells; "stunning quality you'll love" does not. This skill
writes copy that knows its reader, leads with what they get, and backs
every claim with a detail.

For interface text (buttons, errors, empty states) use `microcopy` (if
installed); for plain-English rewrites of technical material, `plainify`
(if installed); for technical SEO, `findable` (if installed).

## 1. Brief yourself

Establish before writing: the reader (who they are, what they already
believe), the one action you want them to take, the product's real
details (materials, sizes, features, prices, proof such as reviews or
numbers), the brand's voice (read existing copy on the site), and where the
copy will appear and any length limit.

Done when: you can name the reader, the action, and three concrete facts to
build on.

## 2. Write

- **Lead with the benefit to the reader**, then the detail that proves it.
- **Specific beats superlative.** Replace every "amazing" with the fact that
  would make someone think it.
- **Active voice, short sentences, plain words.** Aim for easy reading
  (roughly a reading age of nine to twelve for general audiences).
- **One call to action per piece**, worded as the action: "Choose your
  print size", not "Click here".
- **British English:** organise, colour, centre; dates as 4 November 2026;
  quotation marks as the brand's style guide sets them (most UK styles use
  single quotes, with double for a quote inside a quote).
- **No claims you cannot back.** Leave out "best", "only" and "guaranteed"
  unless they are true and provable.

Done when: every sentence either helps the reader decide or tells them what
to do.

## 3. Fit the format

| Format | Shape |
|---|---|
| Landing page | benefit headline; one-line subhead; three benefit sections with proof; social proof; one call to action |
| Product description | what it is in one line; what it is like to own or use; specifics (size, material, care); delivery or returns reassurance |
| Email | subject under about 50 characters; preview text; one idea; one button |
| Social post | hook in the first line; one idea; one link |
| Search title | 50 to 60 characters, main phrase near the front, brand at the end |
| Search description | 150 to 160 characters, the benefit and an invitation |

Done when: the piece fits its format's shape and length limits.

## 4. Check it

Read it aloud: trip hazards are rewrites. Check accessibility: descriptive
link text, alt text for every image (what it shows and why it matters),
headings in order. Check facts against the source.

Done when: every fact is checked and every link and image has meaningful
text.

## It's working if

- A reader can say what they get within the first line.
- Every claim is backed by a concrete detail.
- There is exactly one clear next step.
